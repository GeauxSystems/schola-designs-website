The man behind it

Courtney “Schola” Long

AKA “Value Alchemist”

What the hell is a Value Alchemist?

Courtney “Schola” Long has a habit of seeing things differently.

Where someone else sees a struggling business, Courtney sees buried value. Where they see a logo problem, he sees a positioning problem. He’s usually asking something more specific: what is this actually worth, and why hasn’t anyone else noticed yet?

Frameworks, buzzwords, and 80-page brand decks never interested him much. What hooked him was figuring out why some businesses with incredible potential remain overlooked while others become the obvious choice. Somewhere along the way, he realized great businesses don’t always need more value. Sometimes they need someone who knows how to uncover the value that’s already there.

That’s the work: listening for the thing buried underneath what a client says the problem is, connecting dots that look unrelated, and questioning assumptions that have quietly become “the way we’ve always done it.” His brain naturally lives at the intersection of brand, business, human behavior, and opportunity, so a conversation with him might start with your website and end with your pricing model. Chasing that thread is the work.

Before Schola Designs starts creating anything, Courtney wants to understand what people are actually buying from you and why they’d care. Then comes the part he loves most: taking something underestimated, unclear, or ordinary and finding the combination of strategy, story, and design that changes how people see its value.

It’s probably the closest explanation for how he sees himself: someone who finds what’s valuable, brings the right pieces together, and helps people finally believe in it.

And if you spend five minutes talking business with him, there’s a pretty good chance you’ll hear the sentence: “Hold on. I think there’s something here.”

Usually, there is.

Where this comes from

His path into this work began at Southern University, where he studied marketing, and continued through decades of hands-on experience in branding, music, nonprofit work, political campaigns, and professional sports. Since the late 1990s, he has worked as a music writer and producer, developing an early understanding of how identity, story, audience, and perception shape what people value.

Over the years, Courtney has also worked with nonprofit organizations, including Houston-based Target Hunger, and collaborated with NBA agents. He led the campaign strategy behind wins for two mayors, a police chief, a coroner, and several city council candidates, which is how the community started calling him the “Winner’s Circle.” These experiences gave him a practical view of how brands are built across very different environments: community organizations, public-facing campaigns, entertainment, and sports.
